# Writing Mechanics

**The thin universal layer.** Everything here holds regardless of who is writing, what they sell, or
who reads it. It is either platform physics or writing craft.

**What is deliberately not here:** sentence length targets, paragraph block counts, word counts,
format mix ratios, posting cadence, hashtag counts, link policy, opener performance. Those vary by
account and audience, and shipping one person's measurements as universal rules is how everybody's
content ends up sounding like the same person. Those live in `calibration/patterns.md`, derived from
the user's own posts by `/calibrate-voice`.

The rule this file follows: **nothing measured ships as a rule.**

---

## 1. The hook

**The first line has to earn the click.** On mobile, roughly 210 characters show before the "see
more" fold. Everything past that is invisible to a reader who has not already decided to care.

A hook contains at least one of:
- A specific number
- A specific detail
- A contrarian claim
- A real scenario
- A self-implicating admission, named specifically

**Banned openers.** Each one delays the payload:
- "I've been thinking about..."
- "Let me share..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- Anything that announces the post before delivering it

**A self-implicating hook is the strongest opener available and the least used.** If the post
contains a moment where the writer was wrong, the admission goes in the hook, not the middle. Vague
versions do not count. It has to be the specific thing, and it has to cost something.

## 2. One idea per post

A second consequence, explanation, or example is a second idea, not a free addition.

Two strong ideas means two posts. Park the weaker fit.

This is the single most common failure in AI-assisted drafting, because a model can always find one
more relevant thing to say and has no instinct for when to stop.

## 3. Nothing invented

Every claim, quote, number, name, and detail traces to something real: a transcript, an interview
answer, a file, a message.

**Gaps become open questions, not filler.** When the raw material does not support a claim, the
options are to cut the claim or to go get the material. Inventing a plausible number is the one
failure that destroys trust permanently, and it is invisible at draft time.

## 4. Write the target comment first

Before drafting, write the exact comment you want, from the exact person you want it from. Then write
the post that earns it.

| Comment you get | What it means | Worth |
|---|---|---|
| "So true!" / "Love this" | You wrote for the scroll | Nothing |
| "Great breakdown, saving this" | You wrote for your peers | Some. Peers refer. |
| "This is exactly what's happening in my business" | You wrote for a buyer | This is the one |

**If a draft would delight a peer more than it would unsettle a real buyer, rewrite it.**

This is a method, not a metric. Which comment you are chasing is yours to decide. The discipline of
writing it down before drafting is what changes the post.

## 5. Diagnose precisely, prescribe loosely

A post that fully solves the problem gets saved and forgotten. A post that names the problem better
than the reader can name it themselves gets a reply.

**This is not withholding value. The diagnosis is the value.**

Be specific enough on the symptom that the reader feels watched. Leave the fix shaped rather than
fully solved.

## 6. The CTA is qualified or absent

A bare "DM me" or "let's chat" on every post is boilerplate and reads as bait.

A qualified CTA that filters out most readers is a different move, and it works:

```
[threshold] + [specific symptom] + [ask]
```

The threshold is whatever actually qualifies a buyer: revenue, headcount, stage, team size. Pull it
from `config.md`. Real numbers do the qualifying so the copy does not have to.

Never put a CTA on a personal post. Never use "comment below" or "comment YES," which most platforms
flag as engagement bait.

## 7. The AI scrub

Re-read every draft specifically hunting these. When one appears, **rewrite the sentence rather than
deleting it**, or the rhythm collapses.

**Structure patterns:**
- Staccato triplets (three short parallel fragments in a row). The single loudest tell.
- "It's not X. It's Y."
- "Most people do X. The best ones do Y."
- "Read that again."
- Rhetorical "Why? Because..."
- A dramatic one-line closer that restates the hook
- Every paragraph being exactly one sentence. Some, not all: all is the cadence tell.
- Perfectly parallel list items, each the same length and shape

**Phrases:**
- "In today's landscape"
- "It's worth noting"
- "The reality is"
- "Let that sink in"
- "Game-changer," "unlock," "leverage" as a verb, "delve," "tapestry," "testament to"

**Punctuation:**
- Em dashes and en dashes. A model reaches for them constantly and most people do not type them.
  Use a colon, or restructure the sentence.

## 8. The ghostwriter test

Before a post ships, find **the one sentence a competent ghostwriter could not have written.**

Not the best sentence. The one that required being there: a specific admission, an odd detail, a
number only this person knows, an opinion with a cost attached.

**If no sentence qualifies, the draft is not finished. Add one or cut the post.**

This test fails often, and when it does the fix is almost never in the draft. It is upstream, in
whether an interview was run. Realness cannot be generated. It can only be retrieved.

## 9. The closer is a line, not a question

Ending on "What do you think?" or "Am I wrong?" is a request for engagement that the post has not
earned.

The closer should be the sharpest true line in the draft.

The exception is a genuine ask post, where the question is the entire point and the writer will act
on the answers.

## 10. Small moves that make a post sound human

Use sparingly. Quotas belong in calibration, but the moves themselves are universal:

- **A parenthetical aside that costs something.** The test: it is true, and it slightly undercuts the
  writer. A forced aside decorates. A real one costs.
- **One word in caps, not a capped phrase.**
- **An analogy from outside business,** when the post teaches a mechanism. A metaphor only works if
  it comes from a domain the reader already understands emotionally.
- **Real names, with permission.** Crediting a specific person by name is the cheapest credibility
  move available.
- **Volunteer the weak number.** The number that undercuts the story, stated plainly, buys more trust
  than the strong one.
